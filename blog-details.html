Early Speech & Language Milestones: A Certified Pathologist's Guide (0–3 Years)

Dr. Ananya Krishnan, Ph.D., CCC-SLP

Lead Pediatric Pathologist

• 8 Min Read • Updated September 2026

As parents, waiting for your baby's first words is one of the most exciting yet anxious phases of early childhood. Is your 18-month-old on track? What should you do if your two-year-old only points instead of speaking?

1. The First Year (6 to 12 Months): The Foundation of Joint Attention

Before speech emerges as clear words, infants build foundational communication architecture. The most crucial predictor of future speech is joint attention—the ability to look at an object and look back at a parent's eyes to share joy.

  • 6–9 Months: Canonical babbling emerges ("ba-ba", "ma-ma", "da-da").
  • 9–12 Months: Non-verbal communicative gestures appear (pointing, waving goodbye, clapping).

2. The 18 to 24 Month Explosion: Moving Beyond Single Words

Between 18 and 24 months, children typically transition from learning single words at a slow pace to experiencing a rapid "word spurt." By age 2, most children have an expressive vocabulary of at least 50 spontaneous words and combine them into early 2-word phrases ("more snack", "big doggy").

When to Seek an Evaluation (Developmental Red Flags)

If your child has fewer than 6 words by 18 months, does not use pointing gestures, loses previously mastered speech sounds, or shows severe distress communicating, early intervention before age 3 produces the highest clinical catch-up rates.
